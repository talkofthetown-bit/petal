What to look for

Know the signs.

These are changes worth telling a doctor about. Most turn out to be something else, and checking is always the right call.

A new lumpIn the breast or under the arm, hard or thick compared with the rest.
Shape or sizeOne breast looks different from before, or swells.
Skin changesDimpling, puckering, redness or an orange peel look.
Nipple changesTurning inward, a rash, or new fluid that is not milk.
Pain that staysOngoing pain in one spot that does not come and go with your cycle.
Anything newIf it is new and it worries you, that is reason enough to book.
Petal is a reminder app and does not give diagnoses or medical advice. If you notice a change, please see a doctor.
Questions

Good questions.

Does a self check replace a mammogram?

No. They do different jobs. A self check helps you notice changes between screenings. A mammogram can find changes before you can feel them.

When should I start having mammograms?

It depends on your age, family history and where you live, and guidelines differ. Ask your doctor what is right for you.

I found a lump. Is it cancer?

Most breast lumps are not cancer. But only a doctor can tell, so please book an appointment soon rather than waiting.

Is this only for women?

Anyone can have breast tissue, and men can get breast cancer too, though it is much rarer. The habits and signs apply to everyone.

Can these tips guarantee I will not get cancer?

No. Some habits are linked to lower risk, but nothing can promise prevention. Think of tips as stacking the odds in your favour.
